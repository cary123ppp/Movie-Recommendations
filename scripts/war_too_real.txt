These four war movies feel so real, they're hard to watch. And number one will wreck you.

Number four. Thirteen Hours. Benghazi, 2012. Six private contractors, one compound, and no backup coming. Michael Bay drops the spectacle for pure survival, and the night feels like it never ends.

Number three. The Outpost. Fifty-three Americans at the bottom of a valley, surrounded by hundreds of Taliban fighters on the high ground. [ORIGINAL: We hold this hill.] The battle plays out almost in real time, and it never lets you breathe.

Number two. Black Hawk Down. Mogadishu, 1993. A raid that should have taken one hour turns into an all-night fight to survive. No speeches. No glory. Just soldiers trying to get everyone home.

And number one. Saving Private Ryan. The Omaha Beach opening changed war movies forever. Eight men risk everything to bring one soldier home, and it leaves you asking if it was worth the cost.
